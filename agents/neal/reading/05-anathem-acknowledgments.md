## Source notes

Stephenson traces the novel's ideas through physics and philosophy, identifying sources including Barbour, Deutsch, Penrose, Gödel, and Lewis. He distinguishes intellectual indebtedness from scientific validation of his fiction. Separate lines of inquiry sometimes converged; conversations and manuscript readers also mattered. He identifies deliberate inventions and accepts responsibility for technical errors. He further cautions against equating the avout viewpoint with an unqualified authorial attack on religion.

## Application — our inference

Maintain a clear boundary between research, speculation, and fictional rules. Let informed characters disagree without silently making one viewpoint omniscient. Use expert consultation to expose constraints rather than to manufacture an endorsement. This source establishes his research process, not the scientific truth of the novel's premises.
