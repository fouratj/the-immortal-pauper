## Source notes

Stephenson explains his admiration for a compact comic sentence in Dickens. Exhaustively describing the same physical event would consume space and diminish the joke. The sentence instead gives readers enough to construct the scene themselves. Its pleasure depends partly on phrasing and partly on inference. He argues that readers can learn this participation through reading, without formal literary education, and that direct narration can accelerate a story.

## Application — our inference

When a passage feels slow, test whether a decisive sentence could replace its choreography. Preserve room for the reader to infer. This supports selective compression, not a blanket ban on dramatized scenes.
