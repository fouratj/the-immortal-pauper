# Gibson Manuscript Review: "The Heir Condition"
*April 2025*

---

## The Good News

You've got the street. Javi on his scooter through Quezon City traffic, Rosa counting pesos in a Manila kitchen, Rico with his scars and his knife—these are the people I write about. The ones who live in the cracks between the systems. The ones who understand the machine because they're being processed by it.

That opening chapter? That's the book I'd want to read. Javi tracking Carlos through the overlay, the body camera always recording, the attribution scores ticking down. The texture is there: the Honda Metropolitan, the jeepneys, the sari-sari stores. You can feel the humidity, smell the exhaust. That's the real thing.

Rosa's kitchen at midnight, choosing between her daughter's insulin and her rent—that's where the story lives. Not in the conference rooms. Not in the bunkers. In the places where the mathematics become human.

---

## The Problem

But then you leave the street. You go upstairs. And the prose changes.

Kaelen in his bunker, running models, explaining epistemology—that's not my territory. That's someone else's book. Someone who thinks consciousness transfer needs three paragraphs of microtubule explanation. Someone who believes readers need to understand the quantum mechanics before they can feel the weight of what's happening.

I don't write about the people who build the systems. I write about the people who get crushed by them.

---

## What Needs to Change

### 1. The Prose

Your sentences are too long. Too many clauses. Too much explanation.

**Current:**
> "The nanobot found its target: a smooth muscle cell in the arterial wall, one of millions that controlled the vessel's diameter. Its surface receptors locked onto the cell membrane with the precision of a key entering a lock. Inside the nanobot's core, lipid vesicles ruptured in sequence, releasing their payload—synthetic endothelin-1 analogues, vasoconstrictors engineered to outlast any natural regulatory response."

**Gibson version:**
> The nanobot found its target. A smooth muscle cell. The payload released. The vessel constricted. Damon's heart forgot how to beat.

Cut the explanation. Trust the reader. Let the action carry the weight.

---

### 2. The Perspective

You spend too much time with Kaelen. He's the nephew of the richest man on Earth. He has access to private fission reactors. He's planning a revolution from a secure bunker.

That's not the street. That's the penthouse.

**The real story is Javi and Rosa.** They're the ones who understand the system because they're trapped inside it. Javi knows he's exploiting kids, but he can't quit because he's ₱87K in the red. Rosa knows the contract will destroy her daughter, but she signs it because the alternative is watching Lianne die.

That's the human cost. That's where the story lives.

**Suggestion:** Restructure. Make Javi and Rosa the primary POVs. Kaelen becomes the off-screen force, the ghost in the machine. We see his effects, not his planning sessions. The cascade happens. We experience it through the people on the ground.

---

### 3. Material Culture

You have some good details—the Honda Metropolitan, the Estonian coffee, the Baltic birch thermos. But you need more. More brands. More textures. More of the specific objects that define this world.

**What's missing:**
- The specific model of smartglasses Javi wears
- The brand of body camera
- The make of Rosa's phone
- The exact nanopatch brand (GlucoSync is good, but what does it look like? Feel like?)
- The specific construction bots in São Paulo
- The brand of coffee maker that dies in the bunker

These aren't product placements. They're anchors. They make the world feel real.

**Example from your text:**
> "He set his glasses on the charging dock and watched the bodycam footage download to his puck—the hockey-puck-sized compute device that handled all his processing..."

Good. But what brand? What does the puck look like? What color? Does it have a logo? Does it get warm when processing? These details matter.

---

### 4. The Technology

You explain too much. You tell us how the nanobots work, how the resurrection protocols function, how the attribution algorithms calculate value.

**I don't care how it works. I care how it feels.**

Show me Rosa's hands shaking as she hovers over the [Accept] button. Show me Javi's dashboard glitching, the numbers changing in real-time, the panic as his entire business model evaporates. Show me Marco opening his door for the first time in four months.

The technology is the setting. The humans are the story.

**Cut:**
- Extended nanobot mechanics
- Detailed resurrection protocols
- Attribution algorithm explanations
- Quantum consciousness transfer lectures

**Keep:**
- The effects on human lives
- The texture of using the technology
- The way it fails
- The way people adapt

---

### 5. The Atmosphere

Your world needs more noir. More shadows. More of that cyberpunk edge where the future is already here, it's just not evenly distributed.

**What's working:**
- The MentorMaxx building humming, then going silent
- Rico's scars, the pattern he burned into himself
- The body camera always recording
- The attribution scores as public performance

**What's missing:**
- The smell of the bunker (you mention ozone, but what else?)
- The sound of the city at 2 AM
- The texture of the smartglasses against Javi's face
- The weight of the phone in Rosa's hand
- The way the light hits the construction bots in São Paulo

**Suggestion:** Every scene should have at least one sensory detail that isn't visual. Sound. Smell. Texture. Temperature. The way the air feels.

---

### 6. The Street-Level View

The best parts of your manuscript are the ones that stay on the ground. Javi's intervention with Carlos. Rosa's kitchen. Rico and Carlos on the concrete barrier.

**These scenes work because they're about people, not systems.**

When you move to Kaelen's bunker, when you explain the cascade mechanics, when you lecture on epistemology—you lose that. You become a different book.

**My advice:** Cut 60% of the Kaelen chapters. Keep only what's necessary for plot. Let the cascade happen off-screen. Show us the effects through Javi, Rosa, Hassan, Amara—the people who live in the world Kaelen is changing.

**Suggestion:** Cut the bunker POV entirely. Show the cascade through its effects. Javi's dashboard glitching. Rosa's notifications. Hassan's wheat finally free. Let Kaelen be the ghost in the machine.

## The Core Issue

You have two books fighting each other:

1. **The street-level cyberpunk novel** about Javi, Rosa, Rico, Carlos—people trapped in a system that's crushing them
2. **The philosophical thriller** about Kaelen, the nephew who kills his uncle to save the world

The first book is better. The second book is more ambitious, but it's also more expository, more distant, less human.

**My recommendation:** Choose the street. Make Javi and Rosa the primary POVs. Let Kaelen be the off-screen force. Show the cascade through its effects on the people who live in the world he's changing.

The mathematics don't need to be explained. They need to be felt.

## The Bottom Line

You've got the street. You've got the people. You've got the texture.

But you're spending too much time in the penthouse, explaining systems that don't need explanation.

**Come back to the ground.** Show us the world through Javi's smartglasses, Rosa's phone, Rico's scars. Let the mathematics live in the human cost, not in the explanations.

The best science fiction isn't about the technology. It's about the people who use it, who are used by it, who are crushed by it.

You've got those people. Now let them tell the story.
