# Attribution Economics: The Core Model

## Executive Summary

The Intelligent Internet enables a new payment model: **attribution equity**. Instead of paying upfront for services, people can offer a % claim on future value created. This transforms care work from invisible/undervalued labor into an investable asset class.

---

## The Two Payment Models

Every transaction in the II economy can use either:

### 1. Upfront Payment (Traditional Commerce)
- Pay now, transaction complete
- No future obligation
- Immediate liquidity for provider
- Zero risk for both parties post-transaction

### 2. Attribution Equity (New Model)
- Pay ₱0 upfront
- Provider claims % of recipient's **future uplift above baseline**
- Ongoing relationship
- Risk/reward shared between parties

Both models coexist. Markets price them against each other.

---

## Key Innovation: Baseline-Adjusted Attribution

Attribution is NOT measured against total output. It's measured against **counterfactual baseline**.

### The Calculation

```
SUBJECT: Maria, age 8

BASELINE TRAJECTORY (without intervention):
├─ Expected lifetime earnings: ₱8.2M
├─ Based on: demographics, parental income, location, school quality
└─ Actuarial model from population data

INTERVENTION: Grandma provides 548 hours educational care
├─ Terms: 5% of uplift
└─ Duration: 18 months

ACTUAL TRAJECTORY (with intervention):
├─ Actual lifetime earnings: ₱24.7M
└─ University completion, professional career

UPLIFT: ₱24.7M - ₱8.2M = ₱16.5M

GRANDMA'S ATTRIBUTION: 5% × ₱16.5M = ₱825K lifetime
```

### Why This Matters

Without baseline adjustment:
- Rich kid with advantages: earns ₱50M, tutor gets 5% = ₱2.5M
- Poor kid lifted from poverty: earns ₱20M, grandma gets 5% = ₱1M
- **Perverse incentive: work with already-privileged kids**

With baseline adjustment:
- Rich kid: expected ₱40M, earned ₱50M → uplift ₱10M → tutor gets ₱500K
- Poor kid: expected ₱5M, earned ₱20M → uplift ₱15M → grandma gets ₱750K
- **Correct incentive: create maximum lift**

## Market Dynamics

### Price Discovery

The two payment models create market discipline:

```
CHILDCARE MARKET (Manila):

Option A - Traditional:
├─ Rate: ₱500/day
├─ Cost over 5 years: ₱650K
└─ Zero future claims

Option B - Equity:
├─ Rate: ₱0/day
├─ Claim: 12% of child's uplift
└─ Expected return: ₱600K (varies by child)

Market settles where:
Present value of equity ≈ Upfront payment
```

If equity terms get too generous, everyone chooses equity (caregivers can't eat promises). If upfront costs get too high, everyone chooses equity (families can't afford upfront). Market finds equilibrium.

### Competition for High-Potential Children

```
CARE MARKETPLACE:

Baseline: ₱45M (privileged background)
Expected uplift: ₱8M (still room to grow)

└─ Community mentor: ₱300/day upfront (prefers certainty)
```

Families choose based on:
- Caregiver quality (track record, reputation)
- Terms offered (upfront vs equity %, total cost)
- Risk tolerance (confident in child? take equity deal)

## Remaining Problems

### 1. Deadweight on High Earners

If 30% of Maria's uplift is backpropagated to enablers, she faces:
- Effective marginal tax of 30% (on uplift, not total)
- Incentive to underreport income
- Resentment toward caregivers ("I did the work!")

**Mitigation:**
- Uplift-based (not total-based) reduces effective rate
- Time limits on attribution (e.g., 10-20 years max)
- Caps on total attribution (e.g., max 30% of uplift)

### 2. Portfolio Risk for Caregivers

Grandma bets on 5 children. 4 underperform. 1 overperforms massively.
- Does she survive until the winner pays out?
- Can she get loans against future attribution?

**Solution:** Secondary markets for attribution equity
- Grandma can sell her claims for immediate cash
- Investors buy attribution portfolios
- Risk gets diversified across larger pools

### 3. Measurement Gaming

Can families game the baseline?
- Deliberately show low baseline (bad school, poor environment)
- Then provide intervention
- Claim large uplift?

**Mitigation:**
- Baseline uses objective factors (can't fake parental income, location)
- Multiple data sources prevent single-point manipulation
- Anomaly detection flags suspicious patterns
- Attribution claims are audited against actual intervention logs

### 4. Intergenerational Transfer

Grandma dies before Maria reaches peak earning.
- Who inherits her attribution equity?
- Can she designate beneficiaries?
- Can claims be sold/transferred?

**Answer:** Yes to all. Attribution equity is property.
- Inheritable (like royalty rights)
- Transferable (like stocks)
- Tradeable (secondary markets)

### 5. Exploitation Risk

Can wealthy families pressure poor caregivers to accept unfair terms?
- 0% upfront + 50% equity when caregiver needs money now?
- Coercion through economic desperation?

**Mitigation:**
- Default terms recommended by II
- Minimum upfront payment floors (democratic oversight)
- Worker protection laws apply
- Reputation systems flag exploitative patterns

## Technical Implementation Notes

### Smart Contract Structure

```solidity
contract AttributionEquity {
    address beneficiary;        // Maria
    address[] contributors;     // [Grandma, University, etc]
    
    struct Claim {
        address contributor;
        uint16 percentage;      // basis points (500 = 5%)
        uint256 baselineEarnings;
        uint256 startTime;
        uint256 endTime;        // optional time limit
    }
    
    mapping(address => Claim) public claims;
    
    function recordEarnings(uint256 amount) external {
        require(msg.sender == beneficiary);
        
        uint256 uplift = amount > baselineEarnings ? 
            amount - baselineEarnings : 0;
        
        for (address contributor : contributors) {
            uint256 share = (uplift * claims[contributor].percentage) / 10000;
            transfer(contributor, share);
        }
    }
}
```

### Privacy Considerations

- Earnings are tracked but anonymized in aggregate
- Individual attribution claims visible only to parties involved
- Baseline predictions based on statistical cohorts, not individuals
- Opt-out possible (at cost of attribution exclusion)

### Fraud Detection

```
ANOMALY_FLAGS:
├─ Sudden spike in attribution claims (>100 in 30 days)
├─ Claims on high-earners with no historical relationship
├─ Baseline manipulation (downward trajectory before intervention)
├─ Collusion patterns (same beneficiaries claiming each other)
└─ Time-series inconsistency (claims filed retroactively years later)
```

- Algorithms are governed (slow, deliberate, consensus-required)
- Reference data is curated (fast, expert-maintained, auditable)
- Nodes validate authenticity (cryptographic, not political)

This is the same architectural pattern as:
- DNS (protocol vs. root zone)
- TLS (protocol vs. certificate authorities)
- HTTP (protocol vs. content)

Kaelen exploited the **legitimate separation of concerns** between governance and curation. Not a hack. A feature used exactly as designed, but toward an end the designers didn't anticipate.

The incompleteness wasn't in the code. It was in the assumption that only Damon would ever control the reference data.
