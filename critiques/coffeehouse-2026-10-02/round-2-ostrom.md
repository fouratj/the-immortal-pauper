**Ostrom:** “Hobbes asks how an excluded family gets a hearing when three informal networks control medicine. They may not. Ate Merced’s notebook is no constitution. But your guarantee with an enforcer starts the next problem. When the enforcer controls medicine, who makes *him* hear the excluded family? Damon’s signature rescues Myanmar. I want him to sign—and want the fourteen million never again to require his signature.

“Let us climb from the foundation. Someone keeps water clean, electricity steady, patients alive, and disputes from becoming feuds. Their work creates surplus; surplus permits specialists. Specialization gives the nurse a practiced eye, the pharmacist a sense for spoilage, the fraud clerk a reason to distrust an unfamiliar route. Their knowledge cannot fit in a central instruction, so we grant discretion. Discretion permits error and tolls. To correct either, someone must observe the specialist, challenge her, and try another practice. Challenge takes time, money, standing, and protection from retaliation. If only the operator can afford those, competence becomes ownership of the rule. An institution remains indispensable while becoming predatory.

“Damon understands the first half better than Kaelen. Night staff and difficult patients cost money; stripping hospital reserves can kill patients. Yet Damon holds the Kenya post-mortem privately, withholds it from Tanaka, and places Manila in an appendix. The institution performs necessary work, while its owner makes his judgment about that work impossible to contest. Hobbes, continuity of obligations is necessary. It cannot tell us when continuity becomes captivity. The authority maintaining the oxygen line can forbid a nurse to describe where its money goes.

“Darwin, death is a cruel correction mechanism. It removes Soon-ok’s knowledge of pine bark as readily as Bae’s hand from the notebook. But visible alternatives are insufficient. Tanaka found one and a way to test it. Damon saved her design, sent her away, and found a deputy to sign. What reproduces is the power to decide which rule gets a trial. Variation is governed. The office must become revisable while the competent person remains alive. An immortal surgeon could teach for centuries if patients, peers, and trainees could change a protocol she refuses to change.

“Hume, repeated reliability earns trust, but Bae collected contributions for three years before stealing them. Habit can teach women to depend on the very person who can rob them. Consider the costs of leaving: those workers would have risked both savings and shifts. The deeper question is how an accustomed practice admits a credible accusation against itself. Public records help; so do rival pools, a labor court willing to hear the women without Bae’s notebook, and a rule against punishing their testimony. None works alone. A court with no witnesses cannot judge. Witnesses with no court lose their jobs.

“I would let Kaelen discover this ladder during the sweep, after the revolution run destroys the hospital and before the chessboard supplies the thirteenth axis. He asks why the seized building failed although its ownership changed. ARBITER shows the hospital’s night crew, oxygen technician, and pharmacist continuing to work under a director they trust. Then one technician notices a failure, reports it, and is removed because the revolutionary committee cannot distinguish dissent from sabotage. The oxygen fails two nights later. The explanation moves through each question: why keep the crew, why grant discretion, why protect criticism, who judges a critic when a saboteur can use the same channel? It would make Kaelen’s later plan more difficult without making Damon innocent. He cannot install a culture of protected correction with one estate transfer.

“Myanmar is my hardest counterexample. The fraud filters had reason to stop a satellite batch that resembled theft. Only Damon’s recognized authority could promptly clear it; even then, some transfers stayed tangled. In that emergency his signature was effective and necessary. It did not make his old monopoly legitimate or his refusal to reform morally acceptable. Nor does Kaelen’s purpose make his unauthorized seizure legitimate. The distinction matters because the novel’s living people may need both men’s abilities while retaining the right to replace their decisions. Desperation built some of the pools in this book. Dr. Reyes’s request to Seo-jin shows another source of construction: professional obligation and curiosity, with patients waiting. The challenge is to give those motives room, resources, and rivals before catastrophe makes them the only option.”
