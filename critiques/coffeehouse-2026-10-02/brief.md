He wants as many rewarding digressions explaining civilization as the novel can sustain: what holds civilization up, what work is necessary, what happens when it is neglected, and what supports rules when rules alone fail. Treat expansive explanatory digressions as a central artistic pleasure, not a defect to cut by default.

Five reconstructed intellectual perspectives meet in an intentionally anachronistic Enlightenment coffeehouse: John Locke, Thomas Hobbes, Charles Darwin, David Hume, and Elinor Ostrom. Exactly three rounds, five public contributions per round. Each participant reads all contributions from the preceding round before responding. Round one: 650–850 words. Round two: 600–750 words. ## Intellectual starting points, not scripts

- Locke: property, labor, consent, fiduciary government, resistance to violated trust. Do not reduce him to absolute property rights. Ask who can authorize a change, what counts as consent under dependency, and when stewardship becomes domination.
- Hobbes: security, covenants, credible enforcement, the conditions under which cooperation survives. Do not reduce him to celebrating brutality. Ask how force is organized, constrained, and paid for, and what happens between authorities.
- Darwin: variation, inheritance, differential persistence, adaptation, interdependence. Distinguish biological evolution from institutional and cultural analogy. Survival does not establish goodness; evolution has no necessary destination. Ask what reproduces, what is selected, at what level, and whether death is the only possible correction mechanism.
- Hume: convention, habit, sentiment, interest, opinion, and limits of rationalist reconstruction. Do not reduce him to cynical irrationalism. Ask how repeated practices acquire authority and how a rationally redesigned institution earns practical trust.
- Ostrom: situated knowledge, commons, monitoring, graduated sanctions, nested institutions, multiple centers of authority. Do not assume community is benign or small groups automatically scale. Ask who can participate, exit, appeal, detect capture, and sustain cooperation beyond personal acquaintance.
