**Locke:** We are too agreeable about ledgers. One can itemize every promise and reserve every salary, yet leave untouched the power to close the ledger. How do we keep a hospital open on Friday without letting its founders own Friday forever?

**Hobbes**, your oxygen supplier must know whose order to obey. Certainly. Someone invests in pipes, trains technicians, and guarantees payment when a ward loses money. Others entrust that person with decisions because coordination costs. Those decisions then determine which workers are trained, which competitors can connect a pipe, and which dangers count as emergencies. The office can make itself indispensable. Your reliable commander may manufacture the necessity for his own command. Damon owns the measure that says which obligations matter.

Follow the chain further. Work produces a surplus; a surplus permits stored food, trained nurses, research, and people who can tend a power station instead of growing breakfast. Specialization makes strangers dependent. Dependency requires promises across time. Promises need institutions able to punish a breach. Those institutions require resources, records, and discretion. Now the keeper can price access to the very conditions under which an outsider might challenge him. Eduardo's stamp begins as assurance where courts cannot be trusted. The II rewards it as enablement. Its revenue funds the association that requires the stamp. The rule creates the transaction that proves the rule useful. That is the moment a service becomes a jurisdiction. More accurate attribution may strengthen the loop, because the system measures the work its own rules force everyone to perform.

**Hume**, you say Damon's signature rescues Myanmar because his reputation makes a transfer legible. That gives opinion too much credit and architecture too little. The filters freeze fourteen million people when Kaelen's satellite route looks fraudulent. Damon can sign because the system still grants him an override. If he were beloved and lacked that key, would the batch move? If hated and holding it, would it stop? Winning hearts does not replace an access hierarchy. Nor does obedience to that hierarchy confer consent.

This answers **Darwin**. Death is a crude way of ending an appointment. It removes the expert with the power, perhaps taking the only person who knows how to hold the roof up. The defect is tying correction to the death of the *person* instead of expiration of the *authority*. Imagine Damon alive but obliged to let rivals inspect the II; imagine his security staff paid from a fund he cannot revoke and workers able to challenge him before a body he cannot dismiss. His knowledge persists. His permission to be unanswerable does not.

Do not mistake that proposal for an easy acquittal of Kaelen. In The Discontinuity, the twenty-three paths show **effectiveness** within a model, not **legitimacy**. In *Fifteen Moves*, he sees that changing one side of a hospital ledger can kill a risk pool; simultaneity has a strong claim of **necessity** if any change is to work. In the cascade, he actually lowers Lianne's medication price and frees millions of contracts; those are real benefits. But he also spends a scarce city's worth of power on the inquiry, unilaterally collapses Margaret's prudent pension, and leaves Myanmar's claimants suspended. None of those facts settles **moral approval** of the killing. A useful digression could let Kaelen ask whether a temporary usurper can bind himself before acting: what powers must pass out of his hands as each transfer executes, and who could stop him if he breaks that promise? His Remainders file counts injured people. It cannot answer that question.

**Ostrom**, your circles can replace keepers and appeal outward. But Bae's workers could lose their shifts by accusing him, and a patient cannot exit surgery. Participation is not consent when refusal costs survival. What makes an appeal independent of the employer or creditor it reviews?

The book could falsify my account at its strongest point. If Damon, alive and stripped of unilateral control, still prevents every rival from learning enough to operate the II, then power was not the only thing concentrated: competence itself was. If Kaelen can transfer authority but not that knowledge, my answer is insufficient. Let that be the danger driving the next explanation, rather than announcing that immortality has abolished correction.
