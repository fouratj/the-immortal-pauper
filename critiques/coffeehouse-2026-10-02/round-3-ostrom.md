**Ostrom:** “Hobbes gives me the hardest case. Ate Merced’s seventeen families buy the last six boxes of patches. Six other families need the same medicine. The pool’s members kept faith; the outsiders have an equal claim to keep their children alive. Somebody must decide now. I revise my emphasis: self-government within a circle cannot settle who may be left outside it. Darwin presses the same wound. A group can monitor its keeper and become a more efficient predator.

“I grant Hobbes a binding decision at the pharmacy. I dispute the leap from *final now* to *final about everything*. A triage officer allocates six boxes today. Excluded families can challenge the rule afterward; another body can investigate the officer; suppliers can enter tomorrow; Merced’s members keep a pool without owning the street. These authorities overlap because their tasks differ. If the triage officer also owns the stock, chooses entrants, hears appeals, and keeps records, the emergency has recreated Damon.

“Hume is right that an appeal may be unusable when neighbors expect Bae to punish the first woman who speaks. Locke is right that an employer who controls her shifts constrains her consent. Challenge requires protected income during testimony, witnesses willing to stand together, records outside Bae’s custody, and an investigator he cannot defund. Civilization must spend surplus on the capacity to tell its most useful people they are wrong. Damon spends immense power modeling preserved expertise; he spends almost none making that expertise contestable.

“My strongest digression is **The Last Box**, placed in *The Cascade* beside Luz’s delivery of the nanopatches to Rosa. The page already gives us Merced’s cash purchase for six families; it does not show a shortage or a dispute with outsiders. I propose adding that stress test. Luz reaches the pharmacy as another mother asks for the last box. Why can Merced pay first? Because seventeen families saved together when formal credit was predatory. Why should the other mother accept that? She should not have to; her child’s need is as real as Lianne’s. Why cannot the pharmacist simply promise more tomorrow? The cascade has broken prices faster than distributors can move stock. Who allocates the box now? Someone with temporary authority, using a public rule that can be inspected after the queue disperses. Who ensures tomorrow’s delivery? The pharmacist, distributor, credit provider, and neighborhood pools must each do their part, though none controls the whole chain. Why would any of them risk cooperation again? Because an error can be exposed and remedied without requiring the entire arrangement to collapse or its keeper to die.

“Rosa feels the consequence. She signed away Lianne’s future under pressure. Now she sees that Merced’s generosity cannot settle the stranger’s claim. She might surrender the box, split supplies, or help the stranger reach the hospital. Any choice costs someone; she recognizes a person outside her saving circle. Her clinical judgment becomes civic judgment.

“The novel should resist the seductive claim that hardship is necessary to generate solidarity. Need can produce Bok-sun sharing rice; it can also give Bae the power to steal. Surplus can produce Damon’s insulated empire; it can also give Eunji time to notice a sad Tuesday and Seo-jin time to help Manila adapt a treatment. The difference is whether people can preserve competence while changing the terms on which it rules.

“What is missing from the story is an explanation of how civilization makes indispensable expertise answerable to the people it serves, including those whom its trusted circles exclude.”
