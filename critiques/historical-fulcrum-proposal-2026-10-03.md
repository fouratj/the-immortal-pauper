# Two major decisions for approval: the refused alternative and the power retained

*October 3, 2026. A concrete proposal through the repository's Stephenson-informed editorial lens, not Neal Stephenson's personal opinion. No manuscript edits. The framework is accepted; the new events and powers below require author approval.*

## Recommendation

Approve a documented, narrowly successful AI-care pilot that was knowingly denied expansion, and a bounded but consequential power Kaelen retains during the cascade: founder control of the endowed energy and compute institutions. Let him surrender that control after Damon releases Myanmar. The decisions connect denied experimentation to voluntarily relinquished command without inventing a switch controlling humanity.

## 1. The pilot: competent reform meets a political refusal

**Established:** Bangalore demonstrates adverse selection, cross-subsidy, balance-sheet anticipation, and why an isolated change can destabilize adjacent institutions. The Discontinuity tests economic, institutional, constitutional, and violent remedies. Its new digressions establish cheap AI, licensed deployment, and restricted energy. These facts must remain obstacles, not debating mistakes the new example conveniently solves.

**New premise requiring approval:** Before Kaelen's inquiry, an independently audited chronic-care cooperative completed a bounded trial. It combined existing cheap monitoring and education with nurse visits, supply ordering, scheduling, and escalation. Its innovation removed duplicate institutional coordination rather than claiming nurses or hospitals were obsolete.

The trial enrolled a fixed, risk-balanced group instead of recruiting only profitable patients. It maintained an agreed contribution toward hospital emergency capacity. A funded transition reserve covered affected staff's contracted wages and retraining during the trial. Specialist escalation and liability coverage remained in place. Its independent audit found comparable outcomes and lower total costs for that defined service—not for every patient, region, or hospital.

**Placement:** Inside the failed-remedies sequence, after the first economic failures and before Kaelen enlarges his lever to mandatory cognitive resets. A real archived case tests his confidence in simply improving institutional design. Approximately 400–600 words, replacing some generic summary rather than adding another freestanding essay.

**Concrete beats:**

1. Kaelen objects that the models assume existing organizations survive. He requests an actual attempt to replace a workflow.
2. ARBITER produces the trial report and renewal decision together. The results were accepted. The application failed anyway.
3. Kaelen checks Kenya's mechanisms himself: selection, emergency funding, staff liabilities, and lender exposure. ARBITER identifies what the pilot covered and what expansion still required.
4. The applicants sought another bounded cohort, paid transition guarantees, and milestone-based expansion. Hospital representatives and unions demanded permanent preservation of named supervisory posts, even where the measured work had disappeared. An accredited AI vendor required migration onto its platform. Officials refused new operating and energy allocations unless those conditions were accepted.
5. The cooperative's founders could sell their scheduling system to the hospital. They could not operate the alternative service. Existing jobs and vendor contracts survived; the demonstrated organizational saving did not.

**Sample decisive exchange, proposed rather than final prose:**

> “The emergency contribution?”
>
> “Funded. The staff guarantees too.”
>
> “Then what was the objection?”
>
> “The guarantees ended when the transition ended. Approval required the positions to continue.”
>
> “Even if the work didn't.”
>
> “Especially then. Otherwise they were approving their abolition.”

Expansion remains genuinely difficult. The documented refusal demonstrates a particular knowingly protected interest, not that every objection was dishonest. Kaelen's later simultaneity strategy follows: reforms must move multiple complementary costs and authorities together. It does not follow that his global cascade is validated by one pilot.

## 2. The retained power: a useful exception he can actually remove

**Established:** At Hour 18 Kaelen creates an endowed nonprofit from existing data-center assets. He has temporary heir authority to execute durable transfers. By the confrontation he has lost ARBITER and needs Damon's separate authority for Myanmar.

**New premises requiring approval:** The transfer places the existing operating companies, their supply contracts, and the four grandfathered generating assets under endowed public-benefit trusts. Regulatory licenses remain with the operating entities rather than being assumed freely transferable. The trusts' transitional charters give Kaelen a reserved founder power to appoint or remove their governing trustees and veto changes to admission rules and capacity allocation. He can replace governors who refuse his policy. He therefore controls the institutional gate without personally scheduling every job.

This is substantial civilizational power: he can determine which independent enterprises receive scarce licensed infrastructure and on what terms. It does not control all II nodes, change public law, seize outside assets, or bypass Myanmar's fraud filters. The trusts cannot spend essential-service capacity on his wishes; existing service and supply obligations survive.

**Seed in Fifteen Moves:** Giving away ownership will not itself open access. Kaelen prepares charters that permit outside operators to bring their own models and pursue their own work within published operational limits. He also reserves founder control for the transition. He knows an incumbent board could otherwise recreate the old approvals under new names. His reason is serious: opening access requires governors willing to implement it, and the financial and technical transition will produce genuine emergencies.

**Show at Hour 18:** Kaelen executes the endowed transfers with the reservation intact. Ordinary technical decisions remain with engineers and operating managers; the reserved power controls the governors above them. This right will survive both his temporary heir authority and ARBITER's runtime. He acquired it through a durable charter executed during the lawful window, not through an unnoticed extension of the window itself.

**Make retaining it useful:** Before the confrontation, a trust board proposes restricting independent deployments while incident rates and liabilities are investigated. Some concerns are real; the proposed restriction is broader than the evidence warrants. Kaelen uses his reserved veto to keep the disputed access open while requiring targeted safeguards. The intervention works. Another difficult case is waiting. Surrender now costs him the ability to impose another correction he believes necessary; it is not resignation after proving himself incompetent.

**Pay off after Myanmar's release:** Kaelen executes the scheduled charter amendment deleting his founder appointment, removal, and veto rights. The established transitional trustees continue under ordinary terms, with future appointments split among operating staff, independent users, and public-benefit representatives through a published process. None can be replaced by Kaelen alone. Admissions decisions must give reasons and permit appeal; users can move workloads and leave with their data and models. These are proposed correction mechanisms, not a promise that the successor institutions cannot be captured.

He does not install a single chosen successor or reserve an emergency route back. He remains available to investigate failures, publish evidence, submit work, and argue for reforms. Others can reject his proposal. He gives up command rather than competence or responsibility.

**Sample decisive exchange/action, proposed rather than final prose:**

> “They were going to close access.”
>
> “And you stopped them.”
>
> “They could try again.”
>
> “Yes.”

He signs the amendment anyway. A governance receipt, rather than ARBITER's voice, confirms removal of the reserved rights. Place the act after Damon's Myanmar authorization and before the final coffee movement. The scene should explicitly distinguish the completed transfer batches from this voluntary act: neither the resurrection nor a runtime deadline made this choice for him. Preserve the untouched second cup and the absence of absolution.

## Integration, not seven new essays

Distribute the larger historical progression through existing structures: material dependence in the energy digression; ideological and regulatory capture in AI control; blocked adaptation in the pilot; inherited capability in the chess and estate scenes; exceptional action in the cascade; incompleteness in its casualties; voluntary limitation in the retained-right decision. The pilot and the governing reservation and its successor arrangements are the substantial new canonical mechanisms proposed here. Neither establishes a final constitution or immunity from future capture.
