Dear Ms. Blasdell,

When the richest man on Earth announces he's conquered death, his nephew delivers a hug laced with seventy-seven million nanobots programmed to stop his heart in eighteen minutes. The assassination isn't personal — it's the only move that survives the math. Because Damon Korr has built a global system that extracts wealth from the bottom and concentrates it at the top, wrapped in the language of meritocracy and algorithmic governance — and once he becomes immortal, the math shows 90.7% of futures end in permanent aristocracy. The window to prevent it is forty-eight hours wide, and it opens the moment Damon dies.

THE HEIR CONDITION is a 65,000-word science fiction novel about what happens when a young man inherits the most powerful economic system ever built, and has two days to decide what humanity deserves before its architect wakes up.

In 2050 Manila, Rosa Santos knows the cost of that system from the inside. Her son came back from a residential intervention facility three years ago and hasn't left his room since — collateral damage from a world where parents sell equity in their children's projected lifetime earnings. Now, with her daughter's insulin running out and medical bankruptcy closing in, Rosa faces the same impossible choice: sign another contract, or watch her daughter die. Halfway across the world, Kaelen Korr sits in a bunker authorizing thousands of transfers — dissolving patent pools, voiding human equity contracts, releasing pharmaceuticals to commons — while his AI partner counts down the hours to its own permanent deletion. Every authorization has a face attached. When Rosa's daughter's medication drops from ₱4,800 to ₱340 per month, Kaelen sees the number change. When a 73-year-old retiree in Ohio watches $75,000 vanish from her pension, he sees that too. Liberation at scale is indistinguishable from destruction, and the only person who can complete the revolution is the man Kaelen killed to start it.

The novel will appeal to readers of Scott Hawkins's *The Library at Mount Char* and Neal Stephenson's *Fall; or, Dodge in Hell* — fiction where the mechanics of immortality collide with the economics of who gets to be human.

I'm an Australian software engineer working at Canva. For the past decade, I've built the kinds of optimization systems this novel interrogates — growth engines, pricing algorithms, behaviour optimization — and watched the gap between what the dashboards say and what the people experience. This is my first novel.

Thank you for your time and consideration.
