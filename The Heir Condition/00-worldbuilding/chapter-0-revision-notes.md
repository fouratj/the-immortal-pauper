## What Was Cut and Why

### 1. **Contract Interface Displays (Lines 35-44, 115-137, 406-421)**
**Cut: 62 lines of monospace contract displays**

**BEFORE:**
```
BENEFICIARY: Carlos M.
GUARDIAN: Luz M. (mother)
EQUITY: 15% of uplift above ₱3.2M baseline
[etc...]
```

**AFTER:**
"He checked Carlos's contract: 15% equity, Month 4, eight successful interventions."

**WHY:** Readers don't need to see the actual interface. They infer from behavior. The emotional core is Javi's worry, not the contract's UI.

### 2. **Rico's Marco Santos Exposition (Lines 188-200)**

## The Principle Behind Every Cut

**Ask yourself:**
1. Am I explaining something the reader could infer from behavior?
2. Is this worldbuilding serving THIS scene's emotional arc?
3. Am I showing the same information twice?
4. Could I convey this in compressed prose instead of displayed interface?

**The system should be like water—invisible until someone's drowning in it.**

You showed us the hydrological engineering lecture. I cut it. Now we just see people drowning.

## What Remains (and Why)

The **emotional core** is intact:
- Javi's desperation to prevent Carlos from becoming Marco
- Rosa's impossible choice between Lianne's insulin and Marco's trauma
- The knife scene with Rico (because behavior reveals character)
- The parallel structure (both checking phones at midnight, systems processing their futures)

The **worldbuilding** is still clear:
- Contracts exist, have equity structures, can breach
- The system calculates contribution but workers get paid differently
- There's intervention infrastructure (specialists, corporations, residential facilities)
- The surveillance is pervasive and gamified

But now readers **infer** these mechanisms from watching people live in them, rather than reading the user manual.
