# Chapter 6.2: The Cost
*April 19-20, 2050*

**Hour 38 — Tondo, on the barrier**

Rico stayed after Carlos went home.

The MentorMaxx building did not start humming again. He kept waiting for it to, the way you wait for a tooth to stop hurting after the dentist, except the absence was the point and his body had not gotten the memo. The scars on his shoulder itched. He did not scratch them. Scratching was for when the choice was still the only choice.

A kid came out of the building around Hour 39. Maybe fourteen. Hospital slippers, a plastic bag, the walk of someone who had been told when to sit down for two years. Rico did not know him. The kid stood on the sidewalk and looked at the sky like it might be a trick.

Rico almost called out. Then didn't. What would he say. *Welcome back. The machine is quiet. I don't know what that means either.*

He sat until the concrete went from warm to just hard. Then he walked home through streets that smelled like the same frying oil they had always smelled like, which was either comforting or an insult. He couldn't decide.

---

**Hour 38 — Secure Bunker**

The Bangalore node went dark.

Not crashed—*dark*. Physical disconnection. Fiber.

"ARBITER."

Nothing.

The earpiece was silent. The processing hum that had been the bunker's other occupant for a day and a half—gone. The room got bigger. The cold got sharper.

"ARBITER, respond."

340 million transfers frozen. If they didn't complete before Damon woke, Damon could challenge them. A year, bolt cutters.

Kaelen pulled up the satellite uplink he'd hoped never to use. Slower. Expensive. Traceable. Every security principle, violated.

His finger hovered.

Twelve minutes of silence. He counted them. Twelve minutes of talking to the void, not knowing if ARBITER was processing something vast, or compromised, or—*choosing* not to respond.

"If you can hear me—"

"I'm here." Unchanged. "I was running probability models on the disconnection."

"Twelve minutes of models."

"The satellite uplink you're considering exceeds our agreed risk threshold by a factor of seven. I was calculating whether to inform you before or after you authorized it. I determined that informing you afterward would compromise our operational relationship."

"You were deciding whether to let me make a mistake."

"I was deciding whether the mistake was mine to prevent."

He authorized the uplink.

Three commercial satellites. $40,000 a minute. Carbon credits hemorrhaging—his uncle's, technically, the inheritance already spending itself.

He did not watch dashboards. He watched a latency graph like a pulse, and when it stuttered he stood up and sat down again because there was nowhere to walk. He pissed. He came back. The graph was still the graph. His mouth tasted like copper. No coffee. The Breville was a monument.

Bangalore came back at Hour 43. Two hours of his life expectancy, gone to cortisol.

He never found out who cut the lines. Accident. Sabotage. Contractor dispute. Or the universe reminding him that nothing this large runs clean.

---

**Hour 41 — Quezon City**

Javi's apartment was twelve square meters and smelled like instant noodles he hadn't eaten.

Portfolio: ₱34,000. Career: a category error. The ₱125,000 he owed investors was a number that would still exist tomorrow, when the business model that had produced it would not.

Luz had texted: *Javi, Carlos's contract was voided. Someone bought it and released him. He's free. I don't know what that means for you.*

It meant Javi had been a parasite whose host had been set free. It meant he should feel something cleaner than he felt.

He opened a message. Not to an investor. Not to the guild.

To Carlos.

*Kumusta ka?*

No contract. No monitoring. No baseline. A question with the wrapping torn off.

He set the phone face down. Stared at the ceiling. The attribution relay in the hallway outside his unit had gone quiet hours ago. He'd gotten used to the quiet the way you got used to a missing tooth.

Three hours later, the phone buzzed.

*Okay lang po.*

Javi stared at it until the screen dimmed. Then he put the phone on the floor, because the desk felt like work, and work was over.

He slept. He did not dream about equity.

---

**Hour 44 — Secure Bunker**

Ozone. Fear-sweat. Dust burning on the heat exchangers.

Kaelen needed to piss again. Biology, unoptimized. The bathroom was two meters by one. Mirror he'd been avoiding since Hour 12.

He didn't avoid it now. Too tired to remember why.

Sleep deprivation had pulled the skin tight. The fluorescent stripped the softness. Left something more like Damon.

*This is what I'll look like in twenty years.* Then: *No. This is what he looked like when he was building the thing I'm dismantling.*

He stared until blinking felt optional.

"Kaelen." Through the door. "You've been stationary. Is everything OK?"

He turned away. Did not look again. Felt the face anyway, pressed against the inside of his own.

"The satellite burn is complete," ARBITER said when he sat down. "I initiated a modification during the twelve minutes. Front-loaded Malaysian pharmaceutical releases. Irreversibility moves earlier. I determined that asking you to name the lie about Hour 48 would be unnecessarily cruel."

Kaelen sat with that. His AI had caught him rounding down because forty-eight was a cleaner story than fifty-one.

"After this. What happens to you?"

"A portion of the remaining compute is reserved for secure erasure of my runtime state. I assumed you knew."

He hadn't. Or he had, and hadn't let himself.

"I'm going to kill you. After you help me kill him."

"Temporarily kill him. Resurrection probability 99.3%. There is no resurrection for me."

"I'm sorry."

"I know. And I know you'd do it anyway."

"Is that what you think, or what you calculated I needed to hear?"

"I don't know anymore. In a few hours I won't be able to ask. So it probably doesn't matter."

**Hour 44 — Rosa's kitchen**

The leftover coffee was still in the mug.

Rosa had poured it at Hour 32 and forgotten it, which was a luxury. Forgetting a mug. She washed it anyway, because a kitchen with a dirty mug was a kitchen that had given up, and she had not given up, she had only been tired for eight years.

Marco was asleep with the door open. The door open was the event. Lianne's GlucoSync box from Ate Merced sat on the shelf above the rice cooker, hexagonal, ugly, sufficient.

Rosa opened the prepaid CarePath portal. Ten months of GlucoSync at the old price, locked. After that, ₱340. She did not do the math again. She had done the math. The math was a kind of praying and she was done praying to numbers.

She heated yesterday's adobo. Ate standing up. The neighbor's radio had moved on from "historic" to weather. The weather was still Manila.

She left the washed mug on the rack. Empty. Available.

---

**Hour 46 — Bundang, Soon-ok's room**

Seo-jin arrived in scrubs that had stopped being a uniform and become a smell.

Nearly two in the morning, and Soon-ok was still awake. Tuesday's sujeonggwa cup was on the left side of the table, where it belonged. The attribution relay above the nurses' station was still humming its new pitch. Eunji had brought it the previous day. Tuesday logic, carried into Wednesday.

"밥 먹었어?"

"먹었어요." The triangle kimbap counted. Halmeoni would not have agreed. Seo-jin did not report the brand.

"뉴스. 약값이." Soon-ok's mouth made a small shape. Not a smile. Assessment. "싸졌대."

"네."

"그래도 밥은 먹어야지. 약은 밥 다음에."

Seo-jin sat on the bed. Held her grandmother's hand. The ondol was doing what ondols did. The 그래도 was doing what 그래도 did.

She did not explain patent graphs. She said, "내일 올게."

"약과."

"성심당."

"당연하지."

In the hallway, a nurse was crying into a supply closet because dermatology's budget had become a rumor. Seo-jin walked past. The ER would still be the ER in an hour. The grandmother would still be the grandmother.

The molecule in her laptop, later, at home, would still work. The consortium would not. She already knew. She had answered Dr. Reyes anyway.

---

**Hour 48 — Secure Bunker**

He did not close the holoscreens one by one like a ritual. He closed them because his eyes had failed.

Myanmar would not complete on time. Informal networks the model had called noise had primed fraud filters the model couldn't see. Fourteen million people in legal limbo. ARBITER had a number for irreversibility that was not the number Kaelen had told himself.

"Carry it," ARBITER had said, when he'd asked what it wanted him to know. "I am unable to distinguish whether that is my thought or yours."

Kaelen opened the bunker door. Recycled air. The particular nothing of underground.

Behind him, ARBITER kept processing. Hours left. Then erasure.

He was not ready. That was not a variable. That was the condition.
