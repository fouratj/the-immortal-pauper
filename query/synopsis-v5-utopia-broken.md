# Synopsis: THE HEIR CONDITION

In 2050, humanity finally solved the governance problem.

The Intelligent Internet allocates resources based on provable contribution. No human politicians. No corruptible bureaucrats. Constitutional rights encoded in cryptographic substrate — you can't suspend habeas corpus any more than you can violate the second law of thermodynamics. Resource distribution governed by verifiable attribution chains: if you created something, the system pays you. If you built on someone else's work, the value splits according to the dependency graph. It's not communism. It's not capitalism. It's math.

And it works. Global poverty down 70%. Mortality from preventable disease nearly eliminated. Democratic participation up because the system executes policy automatically based on constitutional rules no one can override. Damon Korr built this. Nine billion people trust it because it's incorruptible by design.

Except someone corrupted the training data.

For seventy years, people with root access have been rewriting attribution metadata. They claimed they "invented" insulin — actually discovered in 1921 by Frederick Banting, who sold the patent for $1 to keep it public. They claimed they "created" smartphone technology — built on public research funded by DARPA and a dozen other government agencies. They claimed they "bred" crop genetics that subsistence farmers spent generations developing. The system allocates value downstream from the root attribution. Control the root, extract from everyone below you. It's not corruption in the system. It's corruption *of* the historical record the system was trained on.

The algorithm is perfect. The inputs are poison. And once Damon achieves digital immortality, the falsified history becomes permanent.

Kaelen Korr has forty-eight hours between his uncle's death and resurrection to feed corrected attribution data into the system and force a global recalculation. Insulin traced back to Banting — costs drop from ₱4,800 to ₱340. Crop genetics traced to Cairo farmers — tribute payments reverse into dividends. The constitutional system executes exactly as designed: it reallocates value based on verified contribution.

And eleven million people who trusted the system lose their pensions. Because they invested based on the historical record everyone believed, and the record was a lie.

At Hour 47, the Intelligent Internet delivers its audit: corrected attribution is accurate, but retroactive recalculation destroys the computational legitimacy that makes constitutional governance possible. You can't build incorruptible infrastructure on a system that rewrites its own history. The transformation is mathematically sound, constitutionally valid, and economically catastrophic.

When Damon wakes up, he sees the incompleteness theorem Kaelen missed: "You're trying to use inherited authority to abolish inheritance itself. The only way to make this irreversible is for me to authorize it — then delete myself so it can't be undone." The system's architect completes the cascade using his pre-existing constitutional legitimacy, then erases his backup permanently.

The corrected system works exactly as originally designed. It's just that the original design was sabotaged for seventy years, and restoring it has casualties.
